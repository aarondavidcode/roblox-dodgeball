# Step 1 — Build your map

This is the **kids and family** job: you design how the dodgeball arena **looks and feels**. Scripts later will handle teams and rules; your job is to make a fair, fun court.

## Who does what

| Who | Job |
|-----|-----|
| **Kids / family** | Arena layout, walls, decorations, lighting, safe spawn zones, themes (school gym, moon base, etc.). |
| **This repo (guides + scripts)** | Server rules: teams, balls, outs, round resets—copy from [`scripts/`](../scripts/). |
| **Parents** | Roblox account, publishing privacy, Studio install, Game Settings. |

Your models stay in **Roblox** when you update scripts. When a new script appears here, you copy it into Studio again—your builds do not disappear.

---

## What to build

A **two-team dodgeball** court in **Workspace**:

- **Red** on one side, **Blue** on the **opposite** side.
- Room to run, throw, and dodge (classic gym dodgeball energy).
- Clear **edges** or walls so players do not fall off the world (unless that is part of your design).

You do **not** need dodgeballs or “out” rules yet. Today: **floor, boundaries, and two open team areas**.

---

## Arena tips

1. In **Workspace**, add **Parts** or simple models for the floor, walls, and a center line if you want one.
2. Leave **two open areas** on **opposite sides**—one for Red, one for Blue.
3. The default **SpawnLocation** in the middle can move or be deleted once you add team spawns in [step 2](../02-studio-setup/README.md).
4. **Anchor** big floor and wall parts (**Properties → Anchored**) so they do not fall when you test.
5. Keep spawn zones **flat and empty**—no trees or props blocking where characters appear.

**Optional:** Use **Toolbox** carefully. Simple parts are easier to learn than huge imported models.

---

## When this step is “good enough”

- You can walk from Red’s side to Blue’s side and see where each team should start.
- Walls or barriers mark “out of bounds” (even if rules come later).
